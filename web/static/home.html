    Data and method

    How the numbers are made

    Option chains

    SPX and SPXW option quotes, taken at the options close each trading day.

    Implied and actual move

    The implied move is what the at-the-money straddle priced the evening before a release. The actual move is how far the index really went by the close on release day.

    Baselines

    An event is compared with ordinary days around it, and with pairs of consecutive ordinary days, so a change only counts if it is bigger than what happens anyway.

    Flagged days

    Some daily surfaces fit the quotes badly. They are flagged, and results are shown with and without them.

    Honest by design

    What we don't claim

    • Weak results stay labelled weak

      A result that does not hold in a second period is shown as not holding, even if the first period looked good.

    • One event is not a pattern

      A single release day proves nothing on its own. Results are medians over many events, with the count beside them, and when many comparisons are tested, one can pass by chance.

    • S&P 500 options only

      One index and one data vendor. Nothing here says a position would have made or lost money after spreads, fees, slippage or margin.

    • Four checks

      A second period added after the method was fixed; ordinary-day controls; vol measured at a fixed strike; and an independent recheck from the raw chains with separate code.

    Findings

    What survived the controls

    Before the release

    CPI and FOMC days carried a bigger priced-in move than ordinary days, and it held in both periods

    That describes what was priced in. It does not say whether buying or selling options paid.

    After the release

    After CPI, implied vol and skew fell more than on ordinary days; after payrolls the vol, after FOMC the skew

    Many comparisons were tested, so one of these could have passed by chance.
